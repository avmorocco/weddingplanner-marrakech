Practical guide

**Getting Married in Morocco as a Foreigner**

Civil marriage, consulate or symbolic ceremony in Marrakech: the rules to know before you set your date.

Key points

## In short

Two foreign nationals who are not Moroccan generally cannot have a civil marriage before the Moroccan authorities. Most foreign couples therefore marry legally at home, or at their consulate where this is possible, and then hold a symbolic, secular or religious ceremony in Marrakech.

If one partner is Moroccan, a marriage under Moroccan law is possible: it is concluded by adouls after authorisation from the family court, and religious conditions apply.

A religious or symbolic ceremony on its own does not create a legal marriage in your home country.

Case 1

## Two foreign partners, neither of them Moroccan

According to a ministerial answer published by the French National Assembly, Moroccan law no longer allows two foreigners to marry before the local authorities. Consulates only celebrate some marriages: French consuls, for example, can marry two French nationals. Several embassies (Australia, the Netherlands) state that they do not perform marriages in Morocco.

In practice, the civil marriage takes place in your home country before or after the trip, and the Marrakech celebration is a symbolic ceremony: exchange of vows, secular officiant, Moroccan rituals, in a riad, a Palmeraie palace or the Agafay desert.

Case 2

## One partner is a Moroccan national

A marriage under Moroccan law is concluded by two adouls (Islamic law notaries) after authorisation from the family court. The UK government sets out the conditions: a foreign man must be Muslim or convert to Islam; a foreign woman must be Christian, Jewish or Muslim.

The foreign partner must provide documents from their own country, in particular proof that they are free to marry, issued by their consulate. A marriage contract, if you want one, is prepared before the ceremony.

For the marriage to take effect at home, it must then be registered there, usually through your consulate. Check the process before the wedding: putting the file together can take months.

Timeline

## When to start

Administrative documents (recent birth certificates, translations, certificates of no impediment) often take several weeks to gather. Start at the same time as you book the venue, 8 to 12 months ahead for the most sought-after venues.

A symbolic ceremony needs no Moroccan administrative procedure: only the venue's own rules (hours, sound, fireworks) need checking, which we do with the owner before booking.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can two British or French nationals marry legally in Marrakech?

Not before the Moroccan authorities. Some consulates can marry two of their own nationals; otherwise the civil marriage takes place at home and the Marrakech celebration is a symbolic ceremony. Check your case with your consulate.

### Is a symbolic ceremony in Marrakech legally binding?

No. It has no legal effect: the legal marriage is the one registered in your home country (or by your consulate). The symbolic ceremony can still include everything a wedding has: vows, rings and an officiant.

### Who should I contact to check my situation?

Your country's consulate in Morocco for documents and recognition of the marriage, and an adoul or notary in Morocco for a marriage under Moroccan law. We point you to these professionals; we do not give legal advice.
